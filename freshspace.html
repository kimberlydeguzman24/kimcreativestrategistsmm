Concept study 02 · Cleaning service · Trust funnel

Freshspace

From "we clean spaces" to a reason to book this week.

Self-initiated concept study · Not a client project

Business

Home and office cleaning

Audience

Busy professionals and young families

Channels

Facebook, Instagram, Messenger

Goal

More quote requests from strangers

The problem

Every cleaner says the same thing.

Scroll through any cleaning page and you'll read "affordable, thorough, trusted." It's true for Freshspace too, but it's also true for every competitor in the feed. When every brand makes the same promise, people choose on price or on whoever replied first.

The bigger issue is trust. Letting a stranger into your home is a risky decision. Generic posts do nothing to make that decision feel safe.

The psychology

What they say vs. what they feel.

What customers say

"I just need someone to clean the house."

A practical request. If we answer only this, we compete on price.

What they actually feel

"I'm tired of spending my only day off catching up."

Guilt about the mess, and resentment about losing the weekend to it. This is what they're really paying to fix.

What stops them booking

"Can I trust a stranger in my home?"

The real objection. Content has to answer it before they ever send a message.

The angle

Come home to a reset.

Freshspace stops selling clean floors and starts selling the feeling of walking into a calm home with the weekend still free. Every post either makes that feeling vivid or proves the team can be trusted to deliver it.

Message hierarchy

What we say first, second and last.

  1. Lead with

    Your weekend, back.The emotional promise. Used in hooks, headlines and the page bio.

  2. Then prove

    Vetted team, same cleaners each visit, photo checklist after every job.The trust layer. Answers the "stranger in my home" fear with specifics.

  3. Then ask

    Message "RESET" for a free quote.One low-effort action. No forms, no price lists to decode.

What I made · Three concepts

The thinking, made visible.

Reel · 20 seconds

"Your Sunday, back."

  1. Messy living room, Sunday 8 a.m. Text: "Your only day off"
  2. Cleaner arrives, same face as last time
  3. Fast cuts of the reset, on the beat
  4. Owner on the couch with coffee, room spotless
  5. Text: "Message RESET for a free quote"

Sells the feeling first and the service second. Showing the same cleaner quietly answers the trust fear.

Carousel · 6 slides

"What 3 hours actually looks like."

  1. Cover: the before, wide shot
  2. Kitchen before and after
  3. Bathroom before and after
  4. The photo checklist clients receive
  5. Customer quote about the first visit
  6. "Message RESET"

Proof people save and send to a partner. The checklist slide turns an invisible process into something you can see.

Facebook ad · Static

"Book by Thursday. Wake up Saturday to a reset."

  1. Image: calm, sunlit room, no products in frame
  2. Primary text names the tired weekend in one line
  3. Three trust points: vetted, same team, photo checklist
  4. Button: Send message

A deadline that matches how people plan their week, and a message button instead of a form to keep friction low.

The path to a booking

From scroll to scheduled.

1Stop

A Reel or ad names the tired-weekend feeling.

2Trust

Before and afters and team posts answer the stranger fear.

3Message

They send "RESET" and get a quick quote in Messenger.

4Book

A first-visit slot this week, confirmed in the same chat.

How I'd know it worked

Measure the messages, not the likes.

What I'd track

  • "RESET" messages per week
  • How many of those become bookings
  • Saves and shares on proof posts

First test

  • Run "Your weekend, back" hooks against price hooks for two weeks
  • Same budget and audience, so the hook is the only difference
  • Keep whichever brings more quote messages, not more likes
